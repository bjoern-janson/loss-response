# Starting literature

This is a seed bibliography, not an endorsement of any one evolutionary theory of depression.

## Evolutionary accounts

### Nettle (2004)

Daniel Nettle, **Evolutionary origins of depression: a review and reformulation.** Journal of Affective Disorders 81(2):91–102.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2003.08.009  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15306134/

Important caution: Nettle argues that depression itself lacks several hallmarks expected of an adaptation and instead proposes an evolved distribution of affective reactivity.

### Allen & Badcock (2006)

Nicholas B. Allen and Paul B. T. Badcock, **Darwinian models of depression: a review of evolutionary accounts of mood and mood disorders.** Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry 30(5):815–826.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2006.01.007  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16647176/

Reviews evolutionary accounts including the social-risk hypothesis: increased sensitivity to social threat plus reduced risky or appetitive behavior.

### Gilbert (2006)

Paul Gilbert, **Evolution and depression: issues and implications.** Psychological Medicine 36(3):287–297.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291705006112  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16236231/

Reviews theories of down-regulated positive affect and the role of loss of control over aversive events and major resources or rewards.

### Hagen (2011)

Edward H. Hagen, **Evolutionary theories of depression: a critical review.** Canadian Journal of Psychiatry 56(12):716–726.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/070674371105601203  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22152640/

Useful framing: evolutionary accounts often distinguish potentially functional sadness or low mood from major depressive disorder.

### Adaptationist review

**An adaptationist perspective on the etiology of depression.**

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25451432/

Summarizes proposed functions including loss avoidance, energy conservation, disengagement from unobtainable goals, signaling, resource solicitation, and analytical processing.

### Evolutionary systems theory

**The Depressed Brain: An Evolutionary Systems Theory.**

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28161288/

Relevant as an example of a mechanistic/computational bridge from evolutionary reasoning to risk-sensitive regulation, not as settled fact.

### Why Depressed Mood is Adaptive (2020)

Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7610949/

Discusses resource-conservation, social-risk, and active-inference approaches and distinguishes transient normative depressed mood from severe clinical depression.

### Masuyama (2026)

Akihiro Masuyama, **Evolutionary explanations of depression and cognitive control dysfunction: A literature review.** Behavioural Brain Research 500:116001.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2025.116001  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41422948/

Recent review of evolutionary interpretations of recurrent cognitive-control profiles in depression.

## Modern-environment inputs

These papers support components of the proposed environment. They do **not** establish the loss-response synthesis.

### Hoppen et al. (2026) — social comparison on social media

Thole H. Hoppen et al., **Social comparisons on social media and mental health: A systematic review and meta-analysis.** medRxiv preprint.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.26363124  
Full text: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.26363124v1.full

Synthesizes 149 observational studies and 77 experimental studies. Upward social comparison was associated with poorer mental-health outcomes; experimental induction produced short-term adverse effects. This is a preprint.

### Lei et al. (2026) — upward online comparison

Yidan Lei, Shuang Hu, Yidan Sun, and Lijun Zheng, **"Looking up" linked to feeling down: a meta-analysis of online upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment.** Frontiers in Psychology 17:1825169.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1825169  
PubMed Central: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13219356/

Meta-analysis of 54 independent samples (N = 36,583), finding an average association between upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment.

### Brinatti et al. — international remote-work competition

Agostina Brinatti, Alberto Cavallo, Javier Cravino, and Andres Drenik, **The International Price of Remote Work.** NBER Working Paper 29437.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3386/w29437  
NBER: https://www.nber.org/papers/w29437

On a large web-based job platform, workers around the world compete for remote work; remote wages were sensitive to changes in foreign competitors' wages.

### Liang & Wong (2026) — technology-induced job insecurity

Jinghan Liang and Chung-Hong Wong, **Navigating the Digital Storm: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Technology-Induced Job Insecurity, Its Outcomes and Moderators.** Behavioral Sciences 16(9):1474.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16091474  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42792274/

Synthesizes 96 studies (N = 43,104). Technology-induced job insecurity was associated with cognition, affect, and behavior, including strain, lower long-term affective well-being, and withdrawal, with substantial heterogeneity and moderating factors.

### Kim & von dem Knesebeck (2016) — job insecurity and depressive symptoms

Tae Jun Kim and Olaf von dem Knesebeck, **Perceived job insecurity, unemployment and depressive symptoms: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective observational studies.** International Archives of Occupational and Environmental Health 89(4):561–573.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00420-015-1107-1  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26715495/

Prospective observational evidence links perceived job insecurity and unemployment to increased later depressive symptoms.

### ILO (2025) — GenAI occupational exposure

International Labour Organization, **Generative AI and jobs: A 2025 update.**

https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-2025-update

Estimates that one in four workers globally are in occupations with some degree of GenAI exposure while emphasizing that, for most occupations, job transformation is more likely than complete redundancy.

## Research discipline

The literature supports the statement:

> There are serious evolutionary theories proposing functions for low mood or depressive symptoms, and there is empirical evidence for several modern environmental inputs that could be relevant to such a control system.

It does not establish any single adaptive account, that clinical major depression is itself an adaptation, or that modern technological environments chronically activate a common loss-response mechanism.

That final synthesis remains a conjecture to be tested.
