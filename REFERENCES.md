# Starting literature

This is a seed bibliography, not an endorsement of any one evolutionary theory of depression.

## Nettle (2004)

Daniel Nettle, **Evolutionary origins of depression: a review and reformulation.** Journal of Affective Disorders 81(2):91–102.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2003.08.009  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15306134/

Important caution: Nettle argues that depression itself lacks several hallmarks expected of an adaptation and instead proposes an evolved distribution of affective reactivity.

## Allen & Badcock (2006)

Nicholas B. Allen and Paul B. T. Badcock, **Darwinian models of depression: a review of evolutionary accounts of mood and mood disorders.** Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry 30(5):815–826.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2006.01.007  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16647176/

Reviews evolutionary accounts including the social-risk hypothesis: increased sensitivity to social threat plus reduced risky or appetitive behavior.

## Gilbert (2006)

Paul Gilbert, **Evolution and depression: issues and implications.** Psychological Medicine 36(3):287–297.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291705006112  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16236231/

Reviews theories of down-regulated positive affect and the role of loss of control over aversive events and major resources or rewards.

## Hagen (2011)

Edward H. Hagen, **Evolutionary theories of depression: a critical review.** Canadian Journal of Psychiatry 56(12):716–726.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/070674371105601203  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22152640/

Useful framing: evolutionary accounts often distinguish potentially functional sadness or low mood from major depressive disorder.

## Adaptationist review

**An adaptationist perspective on the etiology of depression.**

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25451432/

Summarizes proposed functions including loss avoidance, energy conservation, disengagement from unobtainable goals, signaling, resource solicitation, and analytical processing.

## Evolutionary systems theory

**The Depressed Brain: An Evolutionary Systems Theory.**

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28161288/

Relevant as an example of a mechanistic/computational bridge from evolutionary reasoning to risk-sensitive regulation, not as settled fact.

## Why Depressed Mood is Adaptive (2020)

Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7610949/

Discusses resource-conservation, social-risk, and active-inference approaches and distinguishes transient normative depressed mood from severe clinical depression.

## Masuyama (2026)

Akihiro Masuyama, **Evolutionary explanations of depression and cognitive control dysfunction: A literature review.** Behavioural Brain Research 500:116001.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2025.116001  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41422948/

Recent review of evolutionary interpretations of recurrent cognitive-control profiles in depression.

---

## Research discipline

The literature supports the statement:

> There are serious evolutionary theories proposing functions for low mood or depressive symptoms.

It does not establish any single adaptive account, nor does it establish that clinical major depression is itself an adaptation.

The cross-domain step proposed here — that biological withdrawal and artificial post-loss conservatism might sometimes converge on a related abstract control solution — remains a conjecture to be tested.
